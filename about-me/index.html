About me

I'm an 18 year old university student in Sweden. I study computer science, because I like both computer and science... I also enjoy languages and typography a lot. Other miscellaneous things I like include K-Pop (as of recent), drag (mostly UNHhhh to be honest), codes (I know Morse and Tap Code for example), Smosh, math(s), stargazing, TikTok (it's true) and birdwatching.

I go by he, they, or she, in no particular order of preference. You choose!!

The real normal person languages I know are Swedish and English, and almost French. I am however also fluent in the constructed language toki pona, and I enjoy everything there is to consume in the language. jan Usawi has some really great songs, my favorites are probably "kiwen suwi", "likujo" and "tawa lon linja". Generally, I think grammar and phonetics are interesting too.

I also enjoy ambigrams. Most people, however, don't seem to know what they are, so boy let me tell ya. An ambigram is a typographical thing you can do where a word (or phrase) can be read in multiple ways. Most commonly, an ambigram is a word that's written weirdly, so you can turn it upside down and still read it. A funner version of that is when you turn it and it says something else! I both make and regularly look at ambigrams, because they are so fun. If you want examples of really good ambigrams, check out the ambigr.am hall of fame! (I've also made a generator that produces sometimes readable ambigrams)

I'm also really interested in fonts! I'm not a good font maker (I've only made a few), but it's fun to identify them and to know a lot about them and typography in general.

I'm not a great programmer, but I definitely am one. I know enough HTML and CSS to for example make this website. I've also made things in Python, C#, as well as weird freaky languages like Brainfuck and Javascript.

I think there might be traces of AI code somewhere on this site (probably some function in some JS-file), but the website is manually coded. I'm not as anti-AI as many people I know are, but don't get me wrong, generally I am against it. Here are however the things I sometimes (pretty rarely) use AI (in my case ChatGPT) for:

  • Listing spelling mistakes in a document. (At least in Swedish, spell checkers are really not good)
  • Answering a question whose wording is too difficult for Google
  • Making a tricky Regex
  • Translating things that require more context than what most machine translation offers
